More Than Just Food

The Soul Behind the Samayal

South Indian cooking is woven with ritual, season, and story.

01
The Morning Ritual
Dawn begins in a South Indian kitchen with the hiss of mustard seeds in hot oil, the golden bloom of curry leaves, the filling fragrance of filter kaapi. Breakfast is never hurried — it is the sacred start of the day.
02
Banana Leaf Feasts
A proper sadya or virundu is served on a fresh banana leaf — items placed in a precise order, eaten with the right hand, each dish a counterpart to the next. The leaf itself is an ingredient in the experience.
03
Festival Foods
Pongal for Pongal, Kozhukattai for Vinayagar Chaturthi, Payasam for every auspicious moment — the calendar of South India is written in sweet and savoury offerings to gods and family alike.
04
The Art of Tempering
Tadka — or thalikkal — is not an afterthought. The precise moment of adding mustard, urad dal, dried chillies and asafoetida to shimmering oil transforms every dish, adding depth, texture and fragrance unmistakably South Indian.

Know Your Pantry

Essential Spices of the South

🌶️
Dried Red Chilli
காய்ந்த மிளகாய்
The backbone of heat. Used whole in tempering, ground for chilli powder. Kashmiri variety gives colour without excessive heat.
🟡
Turmeric
மஞ்சள்
Sacred and medicinal. Used in almost every savoury preparation. Fresh root used in festive foods; powder in daily cooking.
🌿
Curry Leaves
கறிவேப்பிலை
The defining aroma of South Indian cooking. Always used fresh. Added to hot oil at the start of any dish — never a garnish.
⚫
Mustard Seeds
கடுகு
Added to nearly every dish via tadka. Wait for the pop — that is the moment when oil is ready and flavour blooms.
🔴
Tamarind
புளி
The sour soul of Tamil cooking. Used in rasam, sambar, puliyodharai, and countless others. Buy in block form, soak and extract.
🤍
Asafoetida
பெருங்காயம்
A pinch is all it takes. Pungent raw, transformative cooked. Essential in dal, rasam, and for those avoiding onion and garlic.

Kollu Rasam

கொள்ளு ரசம்

Horse gram rasam — deeply nutritious and bursting with flavour. This potent, tangy, spiced soup is a winter staple and is served as the second course on a traditional Tamil meal plate.

Key Ingredients

  • Horse gram (kollu) — 1/2 cup, cooked
  • Tamarind — gooseberry-sized ball
  • Tomatoes — 2 medium
  • Rasam powder — 1.5 tsp
  • Mustard, cumin, dried chilli — for tadka
  • Curry leaves, coriander — to finish
